# The *Drosophila* CEBPG Homolog Irbp18 Partners with crc (ATF4) to Mediate the Integrated Stress Response in Degenerative Disease Models

**Authors:** Sahana Mitra, Huai-Wei Huang, Rhihab Faruk, Arissa Low, Adam I Yeo, Hyung Don Ryoo  
**Journal:** *Cell Reports*, 2026 Jul 27;45(8):117726  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117726](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117726)  
**PMID:** [42507551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42507551/)  
**License:** CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (NIH-funded open access)

## Summary

The integrated stress response (ISR) coordinates cellular adaptation to diverse stress conditions through the induction of bZIP transcription factors. In *Drosophila*, two bZIP factors — **Xrp1** and **crc** (the ATF4 homolog) — are induced during ISR. While crc was known to dimerize with CEBP factors in vitro, the in vivo relevance of these interactions remained unknown. This study identifies **Irbp18**, the *Drosophila* homolog of mammalian **CEBPG**, as an essential in vivo dimerization partner of crc during ISR, with functional consequences in two degenerative disease models.

## Key Findings

1. **Irbp18 is an essential in vivo partner of crc during ISR:** Irbp18 is broadly required for the transcriptional induction of ISR target genes in photoreceptors of *ninaE^{G69D}*, a *Drosophila* model of retinitis pigmentosa.

2. **Irbp18 enables crc DNA binding:** CUT&RUN analysis demonstrates that Irbp18 loss reduces or abolishes crc binding to target DNAs in photoreceptors and impairs crc's ability to induce target transcripts upon overexpression.

3. **Irbp18 loss causes retinal degeneration:** In the *ninaE^{G69D}* retinitis pigmentosa model, loss of Irbp18 disrupts ISR-mediated protective transcription, leading to retinal degeneration.

4. **Irbp18 loss suppresses ISR signaling in Parkinson's disease models:** In *parkin* mutants (a *Drosophila* model of Parkinson's disease), Irbp18 loss suppresses ISR signaling, demonstrating that the Irbp18-crc partnership impacts pathological outcomes across different degenerative contexts.

5. **Irbp18 is a CEBPG homolog:** Irbp18 is the *Drosophila* counterpart of mammalian CEBPG, establishing an evolutionarily conserved bZIP partnership (CEBPG-ATF4) within the ISR.

## Methods

- *Drosophila* genetics and transgenic lines (*ninaE^{G69D}* retinitis pigmentosa model, *parkin* mutant Parkinson's disease model)
- **CUT&RUN** (Cleavage Under Targets and Release Using Nuclease) for genome-wide crc DNA-binding analysis in photoreceptors
- Transcriptional profiling of ISR target genes (qPCR and/or RNA-seq)
- Retinal degeneration assays (histology and/or pseudopupil analysis)
- Overexpression and rescue experiments to assess crc transcriptional activity

## Significance

This study resolves a critical gap in our understanding of ISR signaling by identifying Irbp18 as the in vivo dimerization partner that enables crc (ATF4) to bind DNA and drive ISR target gene expression. It directly links this molecular partnership to pathological outcomes in *Drosophila* models of retinitis pigmentosa and Parkinson's disease, suggesting that the CEBPG-ATF4 axis may be a conserved vulnerability node in degenerative disease. The findings establish a foundation for investigating whether mammalian CEBPG serves an analogous essential function as an ATF4 cofactor in human degenerative conditions.
