# Sparse component analysis: A method that uncovers separable computations within neural population activity

**Date:** 2026-07-02  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.05.022  
**Authors:** Zimnik AJ, An X, Ames KC, Ulmer A, Lara AH, Russo AA, Susoy V, Driscoll L, Cunningham JP, Paninski L, Churchland MM, Glaser JI

## Abstract Summary
Introduces Sparse Component Analysis (SCA), an unsupervised approach for uncovering latent factors in neural activity. SCA facilitated surprisingly clear parcellations of neural activity across a range of behaviors. Applied to motor cortex (monkeys), single-trial imaging data from C. elegans, and multitask artificial networks, it revealed how population responses are built compositionally from factors with distinct computational roles.

## Key Findings
- **Unsupervised Factor Discovery:** SCA identifies latent factors that correspond to specific computational roles without requiring labeled behavioral data.
- **Cross-Species Application:** Validated across motor cortex in macaques and whole-brain imaging in C. elegans.
- **Compositional Population Responses:** Shows that complex neural population activity can be decomposed into simpler, separable factors that are computationally interpretable.
- **Superior to Traditional PCA/LFADS:** Provides clearer parcellation of neural activity compared to previous dimensionality reduction methods.

## Implications
SCA provides a powerful new toolkit for systems neuroscience to move from high-dimensional "alphabet soup" neural recordings to interpretable computational components. By revealing the compositional nature of population responses, it allows researchers to pinpoint which specific latent factors drive particular aspects of behavior or internal state.
