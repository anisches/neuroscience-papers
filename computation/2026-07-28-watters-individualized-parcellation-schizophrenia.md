# Individualized surface parcellation enhances characterization of resting-state brain dynamics and their alterations in schizophrenia

**DOI:** [10.64898/2026.07.24.740570](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.24.740570)  ·  **Authors:** Harrison N. Watters, Petra Fürstová, Jaroslav Tintera, Filip Spaniel, Jaroslav N. Hlinka  ·  **Date:** 2026-07-27 (bioRxiv preprint)

**Tags:** `schizophrenia` `resting-state fMRI` `individualized parcellation` `cortical surface` `functional connectivity` `quasi-periodic patterns` `QPP` `default mode network` `dorsal attention network` `PANSS` `dynamics` `heterogeneity`

Four preprocessing/parcellation strategies (IndiPar individualized surface parcellation, surface Schaefer200, volumetric Schaefer200, volumetric Brainnetome246) were compared across two independent first-episode schizophrenia cohorts (IKEM n=159, NUDZ n=255). IndiPar consistently enhanced detection of quasi-periodic pattern (QPP) dynamics — stronger DMN-DAN opposition, higher explained variance, greater rank-1 prevalence — and produced larger patient-control differences in static functional connectivity and QPP event rate. However, associations between fMRI measures and PANSS symptom severity showed limited cross-dataset replication, underscoring the fragility of brain-symptom correlations in psychiatric neuroimaging.

**Key contribution:** The study demonstrates that analytic preprocessing choices (especially parcellation method) fundamentally shape rs-fMRI findings, with individualized surface-based approaches better preserving subject-specific cortical variability and improving detection of intrinsic brain dynamics — but also highlights that even with optimized methodology, symptom associations remain difficult to replicate across independent cohorts.
