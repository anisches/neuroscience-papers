# Large language models selectively converge with human-shared neural semantic representations

**Date:** 2026-06-10  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11598  
**Authors:** Chen Hong, Ximing Shao, Gangyi Feng

## Abstract Summary
Using storytelling-listening pseudo-hyperscanning MEG combined with dimension-resolved interbrain encoding modeling, the study compares how LLMs and humans represent shared semantic spaces. Content words from narratives were rated along 10 semantic dimensions (perception, motor, space, time, socialness, animacy, emotion, attention, causality, drive). Both human and LLM semantic spaces explain speaker-listener neural synchronization (NS), but alignment is selective and dimension-dependent.

## Key Findings
- Shared neural semantics are multidimensional rather than a single global signal.
- Larger/more capable LLMs show greater overlap with human semantic structure and NS.
- Largest divergences occur in dimensions tied to agency, affect, and social experience (emotion, socialness, animacy).
- Neural alignment predicts individual differences in story comprehension.

## Methods Highlights
- Pseudo-hyperscanning MEG during narrative listening.
- Dimension-resolved encoding models.
- Comparison across 5 recent LLMs of varying sizes.

## Implications & Current Trends
This work highlights a major trend in NeuroAI: **selective representational alignment** between biological and artificial systems. While LLMs capture perceptual and causal semantics well, they lag in socially and emotionally grounded representations — suggesting limits to current scaling paradigms for modeling human-like shared understanding. Related to earlier papers in this repo on backpropagation misalignment and exploratory experience shaping predictive representations. Points toward hybrid models that incorporate affective and social priors for better brain alignment.
