# NeuroCogMap Reveals Cognitive Organization of Large Language Models

**Date:** 2026-07-01  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00397  
**Authors:** Zhongxiang Sun, Haolang Lu, Qiang Ma, Qi Li, Qipeng Wang, Liang Pang, Chenyu Liu, Qiankun Li, Hao Sun, Kun Wang, Yi Zeng, Jun Xu, Guoqi Li, Ji-Rong Wen

## Abstract Summary
NeuroCogMap is a cognitive neuroscience-inspired framework that organizes the internal features of Large Language Models (LLMs) into functional "parcels." Similar to brain atlases, this mapping links internal representations to interpretable functions, cognitive capabilities, and a cognitive hierarchy. The framework shows that LLMs develop a stable, semantically coherent organization that is partly conserved across different models and functionally linked to their outputs.

## Key Findings
- **Stable Functional Organization:** LLMs develop reproducible functional systems (parcels) that align with cognitive domains.
- **Internal Signatures of Failure:** Major LLM failures like hallucination, bias, refusal failure, and sycophancy correspond to specific disruptions in representational and behavioral-control systems.
- **Brain Alignment:** NeuroCogMap improves the prediction of human cortical responses during language comprehension, particularly in higher-order association cortex.
- **Conserved Across Models:** The functional organization is partly conserved across different LLM architectures, suggesting fundamental computational principles at play.
- **Intervention Guidance:** Provides internal signatures for mechanism-guided detection and targeted intervention for model behaviors.

## Implications & Current Trends
This work represents a shift from analyzing individual features to understanding **system-level functional organization** in AI. By applying neuroscience-inspired "parcellation," researchers can now map the "cognitive geography" of LLMs. The convergence between artificial and biological systems in higher-order association cortex suggests that as LLMs scale, they may naturally develop organizational structures that mirror the human brain's solution to complex information processing. This bridge allows for bidirectional insights: using AI to refine models of human decision-making and using neuroscience to build more robust and interpretable AI.
