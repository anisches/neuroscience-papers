# A six-neuron counterexample to the target-free clique conjecture

**arXiv:** [2607.21396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21396)
**Authors:** Jesse Geneson
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Categories:** math.CO (Combinatorics), cs.DM (Discrete Mathematics)

## Abstract

The target-free clique conjecture asserts that the supports of stable fixed points of a nondegenerate combinatorial threshold-linear network (CTLN) are exactly its target-free cliques: bidirected cliques for which no outside vertex receives an edge from every clique vertex. We give an explicit six-neuron counterexample. For every sufficiently small epsilon > 0, the CTLN defined by one fixed graph at delta = 29*epsilon/25 is nondegenerate and has a stable fixed point with nonclique full support. Its values of q = delta*(1 - epsilon)/epsilon tend to 29/25. In the complementary direction, for any CTLN on n >= 3 vertices, we prove that in the parameter range q >= n - 2 - (n - 3)/2 * epsilon, no nonclique support can satisfy both the fixed-point positivity and linear stability conditions. Consequently, throughout this range, every nondegenerate CTLN has exactly its target-free cliques as supports of stable fixed points. In particular, this holds when epsilon <= delta/(delta + n - 2).

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses a conjecture about the dynamics of combinatorial threshold-linear networks (CTLNs), a class of neural network models used to study attractor dynamics in recurrent neural circuits. The counterexample shows that the target-free clique conjecture fails with only six neurons, providing new constraints on the relationship between network structure and stable fixed points in these models.
