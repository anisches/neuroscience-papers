# Selective GPR17 Antagonism Enhances Structural and Functional Recovery in Animal Models of Demyelination

**Authors:** De Herdt D, Lefevere E, Brouwers V, Hartvig L, Geeraerts E, Hsiao CC, Chen JQA, Pinto R, Duvey G, Burbidge S, Harmeier A, Knuesel I  
**Journal:** PLoS ONE, 2026 Jul 27;21(7):e0354525  
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0354525](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0354525)  
**PMID:** [42507732](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42507732/)  
**PDF:** Open access (CC BY) — [Full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0354525&type=printable)

## Summary

This study demonstrates that **RWT020016**, a novel **orally bioavailable, potent, and highly selective GPR17 antagonist**, promotes both structural and functional remyelination in mouse models of demyelination. The work combines human transcriptomic and immunohistochemical validation with in vivo pharmacology in acute (6-week) and chronic (12-week) cuprizone-induced murine demyelination models.

## Key Findings

1. **GPR17 expression is restricted to committed oligodendrocyte precursors (COPs):** Single-nucleus RNA sequencing meta-analysis (5 human brain datasets, 214,567 nuclei) showed GPR17 is selectively expressed within the oligodendrocyte lineage — peaking in COPs and declining in mature oligodendrocytes — with no detectable transcripts in astrocytes, microglia, or neurons.

2. **GPR17 is upregulated in demyelinated MS lesions but absent in remyelinated areas:** Postmortem immunohistochemistry of progressive MS brain and optic nerve tissue revealed GPR17+/BCAS1+ COPs accumulated along the borders of active/chronic demyelinated lesions. Crucially, remyelinated shadow plaques lacked GPR17 immunoreactivity, supporting a model where sustained GPR17 expression blocks OPC differentiation.

3. **RWT020016 is a potent and selective GPR17 antagonist:** IC50 of 21 nM (human short isoform, cAMP assay). No measurable activity at closely related GPCRs (P2Y1/2/11, CysLTR1) at up to 50 µM. 91% oral bioavailability, high brain penetration (brain-to-plasma ratio >0.05 unbound), and once-daily dosing feasibility (t½ = 6.9–8.3 h). CEREP SafetyScreen87 panel showed no major off-target liabilities.

4. **Functional remyelination — VEP recovery:** Oral RWT020016 (1 mg/kg, QD) significantly improved visual evoked potential latency delays in both acute (p=0.003 vs vehicle) and chronic (p=0.02 vs vehicle) cuprizone models, indicating restored axonal conduction velocity.

5. **Cognitive recovery — Y-maze performance:** RWT020016 (1 mg/kg) restored spatial working memory above chance levels in both acute (p=0.015 vs 50% chance) and chronic (p=0.002 vs 50% chance) models. In the chronic model, performance was significantly above vehicle (p=0.004).

6. **Structural remyelination — MBP in corpus callosum:** Dose-dependent increase in myelin basic protein expression in the medial corpus callosum at both 0.3 and 1 mg/kg (acute: p<0.0001 vs vehicle; chronic: p=0.01 vs vehicle).

7. **Ultrastructural repair — optic nerve TEM:** RWT020016 (1 mg/kg) significantly improved g-ratios (p=0.049 vs vehicle) and reduced the proportion of moderate-to-severe axonal pathology (loose myelin, degenerating axons, mitochondrial abnormalities) in the optic nerve.

## Methods

- **Human snRNA-seq meta-analysis:** Integration of 5 public datasets (214,567 nuclei) using Seurat, focused on oligodendrocyte lineage (71,623 cells)
- **Human postmortem IHC:** Brain (neocortex) and optic nerve from 8 progressive MS donors (PPMS/SPMS); GPR17, BCAS1, PLP, HLA staining; lesion classification per Kuhlmann/Luchetti criteria
- **Cuprizone models:** C57BL/6 male mice — acute (0.3% cuprizone, 6 wk) and chronic (12 wk); RWT020016 oral gavage 0.3 or 1 mg/kg QD, 9 or 37 days
- **VEP recordings:** Flash VEP (200 stimuli, 1 Hz, 0.5 cd·s/m²); N1 latency and N1-P1 amplitude
- **Y-maze spontaneous alternation:** 6-min free exploration; % correct triads as spatial working memory measure
- **MBP immunohistochemistry:** Densitometric quantification in medial corpus callosum (Bregma −2.0 mm)
- **TEM:** Optic nerve g-ratio analysis and semi-quantitative pathology scoring (mitochondrial morphology, myelin integrity, axonal degeneration)

## Significance

This study provides the first in vivo pharmacological validation of a selective GPR17 antagonist for remyelination therapy, advancing beyond earlier non-selective tools (montelukast, pranlukast) that lacked target specificity. The human tissue data confirm the translational relevance of GPR17 as a target in progressive MS, where treatment options remain extremely limited. RWT020016's favourable PK/selectivity profile and demonstrated efficacy across behavioural, electrophysiological, histological, and ultrastructural endpoints position it as a promising clinical candidate for demyelinating disorders.
