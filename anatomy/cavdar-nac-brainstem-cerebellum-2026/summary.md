# The Anatomical Connections of Nucleus Accumbens with Brainstem and Cerebellum in the Rat and Human Subjects

**Authors:** Çavdar S, Altınöz D, Özkan M, Erkan E, Güzel A, Güneş YC, Algın O  
**Journal:** Cerebellum (London, England), 2026 Jul 25;25(4):118  
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12311-026-02060-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12311-026-02060-9)  
**PMID:** [42501186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501186/)  
**PDF:** Not freely available (Springer Nature subscription)

## Summary

This study investigates the **anatomical connections of the nucleus accumbens (NAc) with brainstem and cerebellar structures** using fluoro-gold (FG) tracer injections in Wistar rats and 3-T DTI-based tractography in humans from the Human Connectome Project.

### Rat findings (FG tracing):
- NAc connected to: **ventral tegmental area, substantia nigra, central gray, periaqueductal gray, locus coeruleus, raphe, pedunculopontine, and parabrachial nuclei**
- Most connections ipsilateral; few bilateral

### Human findings (DTI tractography):
- Right NAc connected to: **ventral tegmental area, raphe, parabrachial, pontis oralis, and cerebellar dentate nucleus**

### Cross-species comparison:
- **Overlapping:** raphe, parabrachial, ventral tegmental area, and pontis oralis
- **Rat-only:** locus coeruleus, pedunculopontine, substantia nigra pars compacta
- **Human-only:** dentate nucleus (cerebellar)

### Significance:
Highlights the relevance of brainstem and cerebellar connectivity of the NAc for understanding therapeutic interventions like deep brain stimulation in addiction and other disorders, and for minimizing unexpected side effects.
